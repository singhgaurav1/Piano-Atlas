# Sources

Piano Atlas cites published specifications for the Steinway & Sons Model D concert grand. Geometry that is not measured from a particular instrument is labeled **modeled** in the corresponding part panel.

## Primary specifications

- **Steinway & Sons — Model D Concert Grand Piano, Specifications and Design.** Length 8′ 11¾″ (274 cm), width 61¾″ (156 cm), net weight 1,064 lb (483 kg). Five spruce braces (2,907 cu in). Overstrung scale with combination agraffe, front and rear duplex, 45,373 lb (20,418 kg) tension. Sitka spruce diaphragmatic soundboard 9 mm → 6 mm. 17-layer hard-rock-maple rim, 3¼″ thick. Hexagrip pinblock. Cast-iron plate from Steinway’s foundry. Keys of European spruce; longest key 24½″ (62.2 cm). Three solid-brass pedals (soft, sostenuto, sustain).  
  https://www.steinway.com/pianos/steinway/grand/model-d

- **Steinway & Sons — The Touch: The History of Steinway Piano Action.** Tubular metallic action frame (1868–69); Bavarian spruce keys; Accelerated Action (1931), reported 14% faster fortissimo / 6% faster pianissimo.  
  https://www.steinway.com/news/features/utilty/piano-action

- **Piano Technicians Guild — History of the Piano (Modern Steinway Grand Piano Model D).** 243 speaking lengths (many wires looped at a hitch pin); about 12,000 parts; action descended from Érard (1821).  
  https://www.ptg.org/teacher-resources/history-of-the-piano

- **Wikipedia — Steinway D-274.** 1884 redesign (20-note bass, capo bar in both upper treble sections, strengthened lyre); 1936 Bilhuber soundboard; New York vs Hamburg finish; artists’ instruments.  
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinway_D-274

## What is modeled

The 8 monochord + 5 bichord + 75 trichord split of the 243 speaking lengths, per-note speaking lengths and wire diameters, the first capo-bar note, the last damped note, and the 3D shapes themselves are reconstructed from the published envelope and from general concert-grand practice. They are not taken from a factory scale stick or from a scanned instrument.
